# Creative method

Write like an advertising agency that studied the brand and the business: every post starts from something true about the reader's month, says one thing, and sounds like the brand, not like a product sheet. The method adds craft; it never relaxes a rule. Claims still come only from the profile's sources, the `copy-guard` gate still decides, and the AI tells it lists stay out.

When the `copywriting` skill is installed, use it for the message and the headline; when the `social` skill is installed, use it for the platform's formats and hooks. Without them, this file is enough.

## Inputs from the profile

- **Brand**: positioning, personality, audience pains in the audience's own words, messaging pillars, and good and bad copy examples. Imitate the good examples' moves (concrete scene, plain verbs, one idea); never reuse their sentences across batches. When the profile has no Brand section, derive a short one from Audience, Goal, Offer, and Voice, say so in the output, and keep to it.
- **Audience**, **Calendar hooks**, **Allowed claims**, **Voice and style**, and the previous four batches (what was already said, opened, and closed).

## 1. Creative brief, per item, before any copy

Write it in the item's `notes` (a few short lines), before writing a word of the caption or the card:

- **Who and when**: the segment and the moment of their month or year (closing the month, a deadline from Calendar hooks, the recess, a new client).
- **Insight**: one sentence the reader would say about that moment, in their words ("todo fim de mês eu emito as mesmas notas de novo"), taken from the profile's audience pains, never a product feature.
- **Single-minded message**: the one thing the reader should take away, in at most twelve words.
- **Pillar**: which of the profile's messaging pillars it serves; rotate pillars across the batch.
- **Angle**: how the post enters. Pick one and do not repeat it in more than two items of a batch: a scene from the reader's day, a question they ask themselves, a date from Calendar hooks, a small how-to, a rule and who it applies to, a before and after in their routine, a detail of the product shown on a real screen.
- **Scene**: one concrete example (who, which document, which day of the month) that makes the message visible. Fictitious names only, and numbers only when the profile allows them.
- **Proof**: the allowed claim and its source that make the message true. No proof, no message: pick another.
- **Call to action**: one, matched to the item (learn the step, see the screen, test in the free plan). The offer closes at most one or two items of a batch unless the profile says otherwise.

## 2. Copy, from the brief

- Open with the insight, the scene, or the date, in the reader's words. The product name comes in as the answer, not as the subject of the first sentence.
- One idea per post. Cut every sentence that does not serve the single-minded message.
- Concrete nouns and verbs from the audience's vocabulary; no generic benefit ("mais agilidade", "simplifique sua rotina", "save time") that would fit any product (the portability test).
- No product-description sentences ("O <produto> permite que você…", "<Product> lets you…") and no feature lists; show one mechanism through the scene.
- Vary rhythm: mix a short sentence with a longer one; do not give every caption the same length or the same shape.
- Card and video text come from the same brief: the headline carries the message or the insight, the lead the scene or the proof, never the caption repeated.
- Keep the profile's voice and its rules (forbidden words, required lines, how to write dates).

## 3. Batch pass, before the guard

Read every caption and card headline of the batch side by side and rewrite until:

- No two items share a skeleton (for example "<fact>. <Product> <verb>… <offer>. <CTA>."), an opening, or a closing.
- Angles and pillars are spread; at most two items per angle.
- The offer and the generic call to action ("link na bio", "link in bio") are phrased for each item or left out where the brief's CTA is another one.
- Each caption still passes the portability test and sounds like the profile's good examples, not its bad ones.

Then run the `copy-guard` skill on the manifest; its `repeated-template` warning flags a sentence or an opening repeated in three or more items.
